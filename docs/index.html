The released model has much cleaner timbre — it was trained on ~11 167 h × ~14 000 speakers (paper Sec 4.1). Korean pronunciation is noticeably better on the locally trained model because the released TTL/DP was trained on English-only datasets (LJSpeech, VCTK, Hi-Fi TTS, LibriTTS).

Key fix that unlocked Korean pronunciation

The initial paper-spec port had LARoPETextCrossAttention.theta registered as a zero buffer that was overwritten only by the ONNX-weight loader. From-scratch training silently ran with rotary-frequency = 0, so the text cross-attention had no positional information and pronunciation collapsed to babble. Initialising theta = 10 × 10000^(−j / half) per the paper formula and retraining produced intelligible Korean speech.

What's still bounded by data scale

  • Audio quality / timbre clarity. Our discriminator only saw KSS (12.86 h × 1 speaker), so it can't push the generator past KSS-specific natural-speech statistics. The released model's AE was trained on ~11 167 h × ~14 000 speakers.
  • Zero-shot voice cloning. The architecture supports it (reference-encoder → style tokens → cross-attention), but the local AE/TTL/DP never saw speaker variation. Reaching zero-shot quality requires multi-speaker training data.
